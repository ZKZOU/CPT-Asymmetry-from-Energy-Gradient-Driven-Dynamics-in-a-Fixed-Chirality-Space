## I. The Parity Non-Conservation Experiment and Its Implications

In 1956, Lee and Yang proposed that parity might not be conserved in weak interactions. In 1957, Wu's experimental team confirmed this prediction through observations of cobalt-60 beta decay. The experimental results showed a directional preference in weak interaction processes.

In the Standard Model, parity non-conservation is attributed to the V-A structure of the weak interaction—that is, chirality as an intrinsic property of fermion fields—but it does not explain where this chirality comes from.

From this experimental observation, a structural question arises: if the laws of physics themselves distinguish left from right, does this mean that space itself is a chiral medium? If the fundamental quanta constituting space possess a fixed-chirality ground-state spin, then any structure propagating or forming within it—whether waves or locked states—will be modulated by this fixed chiral background. Parity non-conservation would then no longer be a special property of the weak interaction, but a natural manifestation of the structure of space itself.

## II. From Fixed-Chirality Background to Charge Conjugation Asymmetry

Once the fixed-chirality spatial background is established as a fundamental hypothesis, the violation of parity (P) symmetry receives a geometric explanation. This also leads to a further inference: if space itself has a chiral preference, then charge conjugation (C) symmetry—the interchange of particles and antiparticles—may also be violated.

Experimentally, both C and P are individually violated in weak interactions, but the combined CP symmetry is approximately conserved in most processes. In 1964, Cronin and Fitch discovered CP violation in the neutral kaon system, showing that even the combined CP operation is not an exact symmetry.

Within the SEQ model framework, the origin of C asymmetry can be derived from the chiral selectivity of the locking mechanism:

When spatial compression deformation occurs, the deformation energy can be locked into a stable mass state only if the chirality of its twistor matches the fixed chiral spin of SEQ. Positive particles (such as electrons) have structural matrix rotations that match the fixed chirality of SEQ and can therefore lock stably. Antiparticles (such as positrons) have structural matrix rotations in the opposite direction and do not match the fixed chirality of SEQ. This mismatch makes their locked states inherently unstable—either decaying rapidly after formation or requiring additional energy input to barely persist.

This implies:

1. **Stability asymmetry between particles and antiparticles**: Under the same conditions, positive particles are more likely to form stable mass states than antiparticles.
2. **Property asymmetry between particles and antiparticles**: Due to different locking conditions, certain intrinsic properties of particles and antiparticles (such as magnetic moments) should exhibit small but systematic differences.
3. **Cosmological consequences**: In the early universe, the locking efficiency of positive particles was systematically higher than that of antiparticles, resulting in the observed fact that the universe today is dominated by matter, with antiparticles nearly absent.

## III. From Macroscopic Irreversibility to Time Reversal Asymmetry

In the SEQ model, there is another source of T (time reversal) asymmetry independent of the chiral background: **the irreversibility of energy transfer**.

At the level of experimental phenomena, all macroscopic physical processes are irreversible—heat flows only from hot objects to cold objects, a cup of hot coffee does not spontaneously become hotter, a broken glass does not spontaneously reassemble. This is what the second law of thermodynamics describes, and it is the most direct empirical evidence for the arrow of time.

In the SEQ model, the microscopic realization of this macroscopic fact is direct: the dynamical rule specifies that energy can only flow from higher-energy SEQ nodes to adjacent lower-energy nodes, one quantum of Planck's constant at a time. This rule does not depend on any chiral assumption; it is simply the concretization of the second law of thermodynamics in a discrete network. Time reversal would require reversing this process—energy flowing from lower-energy nodes to higher-energy nodes—but this is explicitly forbidden by the rule.

In low-energy phenomena, T asymmetry is not as intuitively apparent as P asymmetry. This is because under low-energy conditions, thermal dissipation processes are generally weak, and systems can maintain an approximately reversible appearance for a considerable period. For example, the swing of a pendulum appears almost reversible over several cycles, with energy loss due to friction being barely noticeable in the short term. Only after sufficient accumulation over long timescales, or in high-energy processes with sufficiently large energy gradients, does irreversibility become observable.

In the Standard Model, T asymmetry is usually derived indirectly through the CPT theorem: if CP is violated and CPT is assumed to be conserved, then T must be violated. But in the SEQ model, T asymmetry has an independent and more fundamental origin:

- **At the microscopic level**: Each energy transfer has a definite direction (from high to low); reverse transfer is forbidden.
- **At the macroscopic level**: This microscopic irreversibility accumulates as the second law of thermodynamics, driving the universe as a whole toward thermal equilibrium.
- **Entropy monotonicity**: The model's multiplicative entropy S = ∏ m_i increases monotonically at each transfer step, providing a precise mathematical characterization of T asymmetry.

## IV. Combined CPT Asymmetry

Bringing together the above three aspects, in the SEQ model:

- **P asymmetry** originates from the fixed chiral spin of SEQ—space itself distinguishes left from right.
- **C asymmetry** originates from the mismatch between antiparticle structural matrices and the fixed chiral background—the spatial background favors one chirality.
- **T asymmetry** originates from the irreversibility of the energy transfer rule—time has a definite direction.

Each has an independent physical origin, and each is a direct consequence of the model's fundamental assumptions. Therefore, as the combined operation of all three, CPT cannot be exactly conserved in the SEQ model.

It should be noted that CPT asymmetry does not require each combined operation to exhibit equally significant effects:

- **In weak interactions**, P asymmetry is most apparent, because weak interactions directly probe the chiral background of space.
- **In particle-antiparticle comparisons**, C asymmetry is relatively weaker—antiparticles can still exist, just with lower locking efficiency and slight deviations in certain properties.
- **In low-energy macroscopic processes**, T asymmetry is usually not directly detectable, because thermal dissipation is weak under low-energy conditions and systems often exhibit approximate reversibility. Only in high-energy processes or over extremely long timescales does irreversibility become significant.

The model predicts that even at the combined CPT level, there is no exact symmetry—only the three violation effects superposed with different weights at different energy scales and in different processes. The most testable prediction is the difference between the magnetic moments of the positron and electron—a direct result of the combined effect of C asymmetry and P asymmetry, and a direct test of combined CPT symmetry.

## V. Conclusion: From Phenomena to Mechanism

Starting from the experimental phenomenon of parity non-conservation, the SEQ model provides a clear chain of reasoning:

**Experimental phenomenon 1**: Parity (P) is not conserved in weak interactions → inference that space itself may possess a fixed chiral background.

**Experimental phenomenon 2**: The universe contains far more matter than antimatter (C asymmetry) → deriving antiparticle locking instability from the fixed chiral background.

**Macroscopic fact 3**: All macroscopic processes are irreversible (T asymmetry) → the arrow of time naturally emerges from energy-gradient-driven dynamical rules, though its manifestation is relatively weak under low-energy conditions and not easily observed directly.

These three converge on the same conclusion: CPT symmetry in the SEQ model is not an exact conservation law, but an effective description under low-energy approximations. The fixed chiral spin of space elementary quanta, combined with the irreversibility of energy transfer, independently breaks C, P, and T, making the combined CPT operation also impossible to conserve exactly.

The core advantage of this framework is that it does not treat the three asymmetries as independent puzzles requiring separate explanations, but attributes them to the same geometric ontology—a discrete spatial network with fixed chirality.
